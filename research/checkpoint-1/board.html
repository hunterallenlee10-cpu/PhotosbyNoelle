Design Read: Reading this as: a photographer portfolio for athletes, sports parents, coaches, and portrait clients who arrive from Instagram on their phones, with a bright, kinetic, joyful language, leaning toward editorial sports typography, photo-led layouts, and restrained, camera-motivated motion. Dials: variance 8, motion 6, density 3.

What research changed: nothing in the read, two rules added. (1) Every photo stores a focal point so phone crops never cut the athlete or the ball (Adam Pretty's mobile crop lost the skater). (2) UI lives in a bottom band with a scrim only behind the text, never a dimmed photo (Ethan W, Nick Ho's numeral across a face). Also adopted: frame counts on gallery entries, sport filters on the Sports page, no gates or preloaders. Rejected: stat counters like "games shot" (we have no real numbers). 13 sites captured in research/shots/; amycurrell.com sits behind a bot check, so her notes come from markup only. Notes: research/inspiration.md.

Concept: Two Shores, One Lens

Noelle's work lives in two worlds and two homes, so the site has two lighting states: Game Day (stadium night: ink, floodlight white, dugout red) and Golden Hour (sunlit sand, ocean teal, coral), both pulled from her own frames.

One switch retunes the whole site in a single crossfade, and the type changes with the light: Anybody's width axis goes from condensed jersey caps under the lights to wide, breezy italics at sunset.

Sports opens in Game Day, Lifestyle in Golden Hour, everything else follows the visitor's system setting, and once someone flips the switch the site remembers it.

Camera language is the interaction vocabulary, used sparingly: a burst-mode hero that freezes on the decisive frame, viewfinder crop marks as the one framing system, frame counters in big numerals, and real EXIF read from her files (175 of 215 carry it; never faked).

A live Honolulu / Fredericksburg clock ties the two homes together. Everything else gets out of the way of the photographs.
